Velocidad 90%
Cómo suena el español de España: la "c" (ante e/i) y la "z" suenan como la "th" inglesa de think — gracias ≈ "GRA-thyas". La "j" y la "g" suaves son un sonido gutural, como aclarar la garganta. La "ll" suena como "y". Toca para oírlo de verdad.

Acentuación

Words ending in a vowel, n, or s stress the second-to-last syllable by default. Anything else stresses the last syllable. A written accent marks any exception — that's its only job — and it also splits pairs of words that would otherwise be identical:

tú / tuyou / your
él / elhe / the
sí / siyes / if
más / masmore / but (literary)
sé / seI know / -self, -selves

Adjetivos

Adjectives agree in gender and number with the noun, and normally follow it: a small coffeeun café pequeño / a small tableuna mesa pequeña / some small tablesunas mesas pequeñas .

A few common adjectives shorten before a masculine singular noun — bueno → buen, malo → mal — and grande shortens to gran before either gender and shifts meaning: a good friendun buen amigo , a bad dayun mal día , a great cityuna gran ciudad vs a physically big cityuna ciudad grande .

Ser vs. Estar

Ser — identity, origin, characteristics, time. Estar — location, temporary states and conditions. Both mean "to be"; the choice between them carries real meaning.

Negación

No goes directly before the verb: I don't want coffeeNo quiero café . Unlike English, a second negative word is required, not avoided: I don't want anythingNo quiero nada — literally "I don't want nothing."

algosomething → nada, nothing
alguiensomeone → nadie, no one
alguno/-asome → ninguno/-a, none
siemprealways → nunca, never
tambiénalso → tampoco, neither

El condicional, para pedir con cortesía

The conditional (podría, gustaría) and a courtesy use of the subjunctive (quisiera) soften a request. I want a coffeeQuiero un café vs I would like a coffeeQuisiera un café — see the Cortesía category under Frases for more pairs.
